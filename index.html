The sampled counts differ. The recorded source, compilation, footprint and calibration metadata match in this example. Statistical significance and regression are not assessed.

What makes calibration relevant?

QCI limits this comparison to calibration metadata associated with resources the workload used. That association does not prove those values affected the result. Missing metadata is labelled unavailable.

All scenarios are synthetic. The default Bell-circuit example uses independent 1,000-shot samples and saved FakeSherbrooke metadata. QCI currently provides a Python CLI, not a released dashboard.

What else changed?

Code, circuit construction, transpilation, physical mapping, hardware conditions and finite-shot sampling can all contribute to different outcomes. QCI brings the recorded evidence together so you can inspect those dimensions side by side.

Trust and direction

Clear about what the evidence can say.

QCI records classical metadata and final results without inserting measurements into your workload. Distribution distances describe an observed difference; they do not by themselves establish statistical significance, performance degradation or causality. QCI has a provider-neutral core; Qiskit is its first implementation.
