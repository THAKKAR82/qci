qubit /ˈkyoo·bit/ noun
The basic unit of a quantum computer. A normal bit is 0 or 1. A qubit can be a blend of both, shown as any point on this sphere. Measure it and it becomes 0 or 1, partly by chance.
Bloch sphere noun
The standard way physicists picture a qubit.

How the math works

Find one dot in a million.

A normal computer checks them one at a time. A quantum computer makes the right answer stand out.

If both took one step per millisecond. Each dot is 100 items.

And the gap grows.

ItemsNormal computerQuantum computer
1,0000.5 sec0.02 sec
1 million8 min0.8 sec
1 billion6 days25 sec
1 trillion16 years13 min

At one step per millisecond for both. Real quantum hardware is slower per step and can't run searches this large yet. This shows how the math scales, not a benchmark.

What people say about quantum computing

Quotes about quantum computing. Not endorsements of Bloq.

Run the same quantum program twice. Get two different answers.

That's normal. Quantum computers are probabilistic, and the hardware drifts hour to hour. The hard part is when a result changes and you need to know why. Today that means digging through logs by hand, and most of what you'd need was never saved.

      Trust and direction

      Clear about what the evidence can say.

      Bloq records classical metadata and final results without inserting measurements into your workload. Distribution distances describe an observed difference; they do not by themselves establish statistical significance, performance degradation or causality. Bloq has a provider-neutral core; Qiskit is its first implementation.
